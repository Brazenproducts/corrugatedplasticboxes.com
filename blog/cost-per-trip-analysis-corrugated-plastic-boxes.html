Cost-Per-Trip Analysis: Reusable Corrugated Plastic Boxes vs. One-Way Cardboard

Corrugatedplasticboxes Editorial • September 2026

The Right Cost Comparison for Packaging Decisions

When procurement teams evaluate corrugated plastic boxes against cardboard, the initial unit price comparison almost always favors cardboard. A one-way cardboard box might cost $1.50 to $4.00 per unit. A comparable PP corrugated box might cost $8 to $20 depending on size and caliper. On a per-unit basis, cardboard wins — until you introduce the one number that changes the entire analysis: trip count.

This guide walks through a cost-per-trip model that procurement managers, plant managers, and logistics directors can apply to their own operations.

Understanding Cost Per Trip

Cost per trip is calculated as:

Cost Per Trip = (Unit Purchase Price + Maintenance Cost) ÷ Total Trips Delivered

For a one-way cardboard box, cost per trip equals the purchase price (plus disposal cost if applicable). For a reusable corrugated plastic box, the purchase price is divided across every trip the box completes before retirement.

A well-maintained PP corrugated box used in a controlled distribution environment can deliver 50 to 150 trips over its operational life, depending on handling conditions, cleaning frequency, and load type. For the purposes of this model, we'll use a conservative 60-trip assumption.

Sample Cost-Per-Trip Calculation

Assumptions for a mid-size distribution operation:

  • Box size: 600mm x 400mm x 300mm (standard produce or parts footprint)
  • Cardboard box cost: $2.50 per unit (one way, no deposit)
  • PP corrugated box cost: $14.00 per unit
  • Washing cost per trip (PP corrugated): $0.15
  • PP corrugated trip life: 60 trips

Cardboard cost per trip: $2.50

PP corrugated cost per trip: ($14.00 ÷ 60) + $0.15 = $0.23 + $0.15 = $0.38 per trip

At 60 trips, the PP corrugated box costs 85% less per trip than cardboard. Even at 20 trips — a very conservative estimate for a well-run program — the PP box at $0.85 per trip still costs 66% less than cardboard.

Additional Cost Factors Often Overlooked

The basic model above understates the total advantage of reusable PP corrugated boxes because it doesn't capture several downstream costs:

  • Cardboard disposal: Baling and hauling used cardboard has a real cost — typically $0.05 to $0.25 per box depending on volume and location. Some operations pay a service provider for corrugated disposal during peak seasons.
  • Product damage: Wet cardboard collapse is a leading cause of in-transit product damage claims. The cost of damaged goods — even if partially recovered through insurance — should be factored into the packaging cost model.
  • Labor for box assembly: One-way cardboard boxes require setup time per use. PP corrugated fold-up boxes also require assembly, but are often designed for faster setup with fewer parts and no need for tape.
  • Storage footprint: PP corrugated boxes typically nest or stack flat when empty, reducing warehouse storage cost per box relative to pre-assembled cardboard.

Break-Even Analysis

A simple break-even calculation shows at what trip count the PP corrugated box becomes cheaper than cardboard:

Break-even trips = PP Unit Cost ÷ (Cardboard Cost Per Trip - Wash Cost Per Trip)

Using the example above: $14.00 ÷ ($2.50 - $0.15) = 5.96 trips

After just 6 trips, the reusable PP corrugated box has already paid for itself relative to disposable cardboard. Every subsequent trip is pure cost savings.

Closed-Loop vs. Open-Loop Distribution

The cost-per-trip model works best in closed-loop distribution — where boxes travel from a central origin to defined points and return for reuse. Examples include manufacturer-to-retailer, food processor-to-distribution center, and parts supplier-to-assembly plant networks.

Open-loop distribution, where boxes are not returned, eliminates the reuse advantage. However, even in partially open loops — where 70–80% of boxes return — the economics still typically favor PP corrugated over cardboard for medium-to-high volume operations.

Making the Case Internally

When presenting the switch from cardboard to reusable PP corrugated to finance or operations leadership, lead with the cost-per-trip model and break-even analysis. Use your actual box costs, trip counts, and disposal expenses to build a site-specific model. Most operations find break-even at 5–10 trips, with full annual savings becoming visible within the first year of a properly managed reusable packaging program.
