The Custom Order Process for Corrugated Plastic Boxes: A Buyer's Walkthrough

Corrugatedplasticboxes Editorial • October 2026

Custom Is the Norm, Not the Exception

Most buyers assume a custom corrugated plastic box means long delays and painful minimums. In practice, when you work directly with a manufacturer, the custom process is structured, predictable, and faster than many expect. The key is understanding each stage so you can prepare the right information up front and avoid the back-and-forth that stretches timelines. This walkthrough maps the journey from first inquiry to repeat order.

Step 1: Define the Application

Before dimensions, define the job. What product goes in the box? What is the weight per box and the stack height on a pallet? Is it a closed-loop reusable program or one-way shipping? Will it see wash-down, cold storage, or automated handling? These answers drive resin grade, wall thickness, and flute profile. Elipacko's corrugated plastic boxes are engineered around the application, so arriving with clear use-case data shortens the entire process.

Step 2: Specify Dimensions and Features

Provide internal dimensions (driven by product fit) and confirm the external footprint against your pallet pattern. Specify closures — hinged lids, tuck flaps, die-cut handles — and any inserts or dividers. Decide on printing: company marks, handling icons, or lot-code areas. If you are not sure what is feasible, a manufacturer's engineering team can advise. Elipacko's custom PP packaging boxes support a wide range of closures, print, and insert options within a single production run.

Step 3: Quoting and Material Confirmation

With specs in hand, the manufacturer quotes per-unit price, tooling cost, and minimum order quantity. This is the stage to lock in FDA-compliant resin documentation if the box contacts food, and to confirm country of origin and duty status. Elipacko manufactures in Thailand and Vietnam — outside US antidumping duty orders, a 0% ADD position — which should be confirmed in writing at quote stage so landed cost is clear.

Step 4: Sampling and Approval

Before committing to full production, request a sample or prototype. This lets your team validate fit with actual product, test stacking strength, and confirm the box runs through your handling equipment. Approve the sample formally, including print proofs, so the production run matches expectations exactly.

Step 5: Tooling and Production

Once approved, tooling is cut (a one-time cost you own going forward) and production scheduled. Lead times depend on volume and complexity, but a direct manufacturer gives you visibility into the production calendar. For seasonal demand, place the order against a forecast so inventory lands before peak.

Step 6: Reorder and Iteration

The real payoff of the custom process comes at reorder. With tooling already in place and specs documented, repeat orders are fast and consistent. If the application evolves, minor spec changes are far easier against an existing relationship than starting from a blank catalog.

Preparing for a Smooth Order

  • Document product weight, stack height, and handling environment
  • Confirm internal dimensions and pallet-matched external footprint
  • Decide closures, inserts, and print requirements early
  • Request FDA-compliance and country-of-origin documentation at quote
  • Always validate a physical sample before production

Direct Is Faster

The custom order process is only slow when it runs through intermediaries. Working direct with a manufacturer who controls resin, tooling, and scheduling compresses each step. Elipacko offers US-available custom corrugated plastic boxes with FDA-compliant resin and a 0% duty origin, and US manufacturing capacity is in development to further shorten domestic lead times. For procurement teams, understanding the six stages above turns custom packaging from a perceived hassle into a repeatable, well-controlled supply process.
