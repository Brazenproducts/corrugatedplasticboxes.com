How to Order Custom Corrugated Plastic Boxes: A Procurement Guide

Corrugatedplasticboxes Editorial • September 2026

Why Custom Sizing Matters for Corrugated Plastic Boxes

Standard off-the-shelf corrugated plastic boxes cover a wide range of common applications, but many operations — particularly in manufacturing, food processing, and distribution — require dimensions that match a specific product footprint, conveyor width, racking bay, or pallet configuration. Custom corrugated plastic boxes eliminate wasted space, reduce product movement during transit, and often result in a lower cost-per-unit when ordered at the right volume.

This guide walks procurement managers through the specification and ordering process for custom twin-wall PP corrugated boxes, from initial spec sheet to first shipment.

Step 1: Define Your Box Dimensions

Start with the internal dimensions your product requires — length, width, and depth — measured in millimeters or inches. If the box will be loaded on a standard pallet, verify that your footprint tiles efficiently on either a 48" x 40" GMA pallet or a Euro 1200mm x 800mm pallet. Wasted pallet space directly increases freight cost per unit.

Also specify whether dimensions are interior or exterior. Wall caliper (see Step 2) affects the difference between interior and exterior dimensions, so aligning on this early avoids revision cycles.

Step 2: Select Wall Caliper and Flute Type

Twin-wall PP corrugated is available in several standard calipers:

  • 2mm: Lightweight applications, dividers, layer pads, light-duty inserts
  • 4mm: General-purpose packaging, parts trays, medium-weight products
  • 6mm: Heavy-duty boxes, produce packaging, industrial distribution containers
  • 8mm or 10mm: Bulk containers, gaylord-style boxes, high-stacking applications

Caliper selection should be informed by stacking load requirements and the number of trip cycles expected per year. Your supplier should be able to provide static compression data for your selected caliper at your expected stacking height.

Step 3: Choose Color, Print, and Finish

PP corrugated is available in a wide range of standard colors including black, white, grey, blue, green, yellow, and red. Color coding by product line, department, or customer is a common and cost-effective way to improve sorting accuracy in distribution environments.

Print options include screen printing (one to four colors) for logos, part numbers, directional arrows, and barcodes. For food-grade applications, confirm that any inks used are food-safe and will not transfer to product surfaces. Printed boxes typically require a minimum order quantity (MOQ) above blank boxes due to setup costs.

Step 4: Specify Box Style and Closure Type

The most common corrugated plastic box styles are:

  • Die-cut fold-up: Ships flat, assembles without tools or fasteners, most economical to produce and ship
  • Hinged-lid: One-piece construction with an attached lid, suitable for reusable transit packaging
  • Attached-lid container (ALC): Hinged lid integrated into the base, provides secure closure without separate lids
  • Open-top tray or tote: No lid required, used for parts presentation, pick bins, and assembly flow

Closure options include tuck flaps, hook-and-loop fasteners, cable ties, and custom latches. For heavy-duty or high-cycle applications, reinforced corner construction and stitching or riveting at stress points is available.

Step 5: Confirm FDA Compliance Requirements

If the boxes will contact food products directly, specify FDA-compliant resin. Not all PP corrugated sheet stock is manufactured with food-contact-grade resin, and the distinction matters for regulatory compliance, audit documentation, and retail customer requirements. Request resin certification documentation from your supplier before finalizing a purchase order.

Step 6: Understand MOQ and Lead Times

Custom corrugated plastic boxes typically require a die or cutting tool for the specific dimensions, which represents a one-time tooling cost that is amortized across the order quantity. Standard MOQs for custom die-cut boxes range from 500 to 2,000 units depending on complexity, with larger volumes reducing per-unit cost significantly.

Lead times for custom orders from overseas manufacturers — particularly those in Thailand and Vietnam producing at 0% anti-dumping duty for the US market — typically run 30 to 60 days from order confirmation to port of export, plus transit time. For buyers with reliable demand, placing blanket orders with scheduled releases is an effective strategy to maintain availability without carrying excess inventory.

Checklist: What to Include in Your Custom Box Specification

  • Interior and exterior dimensions (L x W x D)
  • Wall caliper required (2mm, 4mm, 6mm, 8mm)
  • Quantity per order and expected annual volume
  • Color (from standard range or custom match)
  • Print requirements (logo, text, barcodes)
  • FDA or food-contact compliance requirement (yes/no)
  • Closure style and assembly method
  • Stacking load and trip cycle requirements
  • Delivery port or warehouse destination
